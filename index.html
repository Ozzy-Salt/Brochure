CRI · Ra 0–100

Colour Rendering Index

How accurately a source reveals the true colour of what it lights. Critical wherever material, art, food or skin is on show — and the first spec we refuse to compromise.

UGR · ≤ 19 / ≤ 16

Unified Glare Rating

An international measure of discomfort glare, comparing each fitting's luminance against the background the eye actually sees. Offices and classrooms target 19 or below; visually critical rooms, 16.

Lumens · lm

Total light output

The quantity of visible light a source emits. Higher output looks brighter — but placement, beam and surface finish decide whether that brightness becomes comfort or glare.

TRU Colour

Over a billion wavelengths, tuned to the red and green bands that most LEDs give up on.

Those are the wavelengths that are difficult to attain and the ones the eye is least willing to forgive. Getting them right is what makes food look like food and skin look like skin — and it is the difference between a room that is bright and a room that feels well.

04 — Circadian lighting 24 hours

A building can keep time with you.

All living things — including plants — use circadian rhythms to regulate their internal cycles.

Light, natural or artificial, is the single biggest influence on that rhythm. For a flower it decides when the petals open in the sun and close again at night. For a person it is the sleep-wake cycle that keeps you alert and focused through the day and lets you rest after dark.

Which means a lighting scheme is never neutral. Get it wrong and a building works against the people inside it; tune it, and the light shifts through the day the way daylight would.

  • 06:00 Morning Cool light, high intensity — supporting the cortisol rise that wakes you.
  • 12:00 Afternoon Cool light, medium intensity — the window for focus and coordination.
  • 18:00 Evening Warm light, low intensity — winding down as digestion slows.
  • 00:00 Midnight No light — body temperature at its lowest.
05 — Lighting types Applied 3000K

Task

Precise, functional illumination shaped around a specific activity — comfort and efficiency held without losing the aesthetic of the room.

Accent

Used to lift a single element — artwork, a material, a structural move — adding depth and creating the focal points a space is read by.

Wall grazers

Slim linear fittings casting light tight along a surface, so texture and detail come forward as shadow and depth rather than flat brightness.

Wall washers

An even, uniform wash across a vertical plane — the quietest way to make a room feel larger and to hold artwork in consistent light.
